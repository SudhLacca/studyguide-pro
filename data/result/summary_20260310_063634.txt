A novel hybrid technique for document forgery detection using Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) has been proposed. The method treats forgery detection as an image segmentation task, leveraging the capabilities of CNNs to accurately identify and delineate suspicious regions within any type of document, whether digital or handwritten. The proposed method uses a multi-task learning approach, where the model is trained to perform both image and text-based forgery detection tasks simultaneously. This allows the model to learn patterns related to both text and forgery, enabling it to detect sophisticated forgeries. The model is designed to be adaptable to a wide range of documents, including digital and paper-based documents. It uses both images and text to detect tampered visual parts, such as photos or stamps, while also identifying text anomalies, such as altered characters or forged signatures. The model's ability to segment a document into meaningful segments enables it to analyze the document more accurately, especially when differences between the original and forged parts of a document are minimal. The model also provides adequate accuracy for fine-grained manipulations at low resolutions or degraded images. The proposed method has several advantages, including: * Enhanced precision for forgery detection * Ability to detect sophisticated forgeries * Adaptability to a wide range of documents * Ability to segment documents into meaningful segments * Adequate accuracy for fine-grained manipulations However, the method also has some limitations, including: * Need for large datasets for training * Difficulty in detecting minute and localized regions of forgery * Limited robustness to distortion effects and cross-domain generalization To address these limitations, several techniques have been proposed, including: * Incorporating an auxiliary text detection task using a pre-trained text detection model * Using Optical Character Recognition (OCR) for consistency checks * Incorporating deep learning models, such as Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), to simulate forgery techniques and produce synthetic forgeries * Using attention mechanisms to narrow down the focus to parts of a document that are most relevant * Combining the detection with Explainable AI (XAI) techniques to provide transparency in the process The proposed method is expected to have several applications, including: * Forensic analysis * Document verification * Anti-money laundering * Identity verification Overall, the proposed hybrid technique for document forgery detection using CNNs and multi-task learning has the potential to significantly improve the accuracy and robustness of forgery detection in various applications.
